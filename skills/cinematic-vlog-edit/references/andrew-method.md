# Andrew's simple cinematic method

Source: Andrew (@andrews_life, 230K subs), "The Power of SIMPLE Editing"
(youtu.be/pdLEHfkwgV8, Sep 2025). He credits Casey Neistat, Gawx Art, Sam Kolder,
SleepyCharlie, Life of Riza and Jake Frew as influences. Paraphrased, plus
observations from the storyboard frames (one about every 5 s).

## 1. Shot sequence

- A well-known technique; Andrew didn't invent it. It does two jobs at once: moves
  the story forward quickly, and hooks ("you're still here, aren't you?").
- **Recipe:**
  1. Film a few clips of *one action or routine*.
  2. Cut every clip to the **same length**.
  3. Stitch them so they **flow together in motion**. Match the movement direction
     across the cut: a hand moving right exits, and the next clip's motion continues
     right.
- His opener: a drawer pull → brushing teeth (two angles) → a paper note being taped
  → picking a shirt → a top-down shot sitting at the desk. Each clip is about 1 s.
  The "clip 1 / clip 2 / clip 3" note on a cutting mat explains the concept on
  screen.
- **Agent heuristic:** 4–8 clips, 0.6–1.5 s each, or exactly N beats of the track.

## 2. Sound design

- Visuals are only half the picture; the sequence feels unfinished without sound.
- Andrew shows the names of the SFX on screen. The two readable in the frames are
  **"flute riser"** (building into the sequence) and **"air passing"** (a whoosh on
  movement), each shown as a green waveform tile with its name over the footage.
- **Pattern:** a riser before the sequence or reveal, an air-passing whoosh on every
  whip pan or fast move, and real foley on hand actions (drawer, toothbrush, tape,
  fabric).
- (The storyboard only samples every ~5 s, so other SFX names he shows may be
  missing from this list.)

## 3. Music

- The backbone: it sets rhythm, pace, emotion and story.
- **Avoid lo-fi** (stale, overused). Use **jazz, house or cinematic** music that can
  tell the story alongside the visuals.
- Finding fitting music is a skill and takes time, so **choose it before filming**.
  Then you know the rhythm you're shooting for.
- **BPM** tells you how fast the song moves, so you can pace the clip lengths.
- **Dips** (where the song drops out or breaks down) are where scene changes can
  happen naturally, instead of chopping the music up in an unnatural way.

## 4. Colour grading: the flavour

- The bread analogy: gather the ingredients, bake, then add flavour (the grade).
- The grade carries the whole aesthetic, and now more than ever it is what stops the
  scroll.
- **His steps:**
  1. **Add light.** More light = more information to work with in the grade.
  2. **Practical lights** in the background (a globe lamp, a warm lamp) for pop.
  3. **Colourful objects** in the frame.
  4. **A one-click grade** (his LUT) in the edit. Fast and consistent.
- **Look observed:** warm amber tungsten interiors, deep but not crushed shadows,
  skin kept natural, gentle teal in the shadows. Wide-angle lens with heavy barrel
  distortion and a vignette; handheld, close, sometimes a fisheye-style low angle.
  Frames often have the camera very near his face, plus mid-wides of the room.

## 5. Structure and packaging

- **Short and direct.** About 5 minutes, informative and straight to the point.
- His challenge to viewers: one story → **3 chapters** → each chapter gets a visual
  sequence paired with **2–3 songs** in total, plus aesthetic framing so the audience
  feels "this is beautiful".
- **End by talking through the process**, so the audience feels part of the creative
  process.
- **Retention CTA:** "if you made it this far you're part of the 20 %".
- **Occasional motion graphics:** a blueprint-paper explainer (hand-lettered labels
  "VIRALITY", "DISTRIBUTION", "THE CREATOR ECONOMY" on a blue grid with props like a
  coffee cup and a $100 bill), a camcorder REC-frame overlay with a two-line caption,
  and a small "ONE CLICK GRADE" label tag. Otherwise the video is mostly
  footage-driven with very few captions.
