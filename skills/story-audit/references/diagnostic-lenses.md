# Diagnostic lenses

Work through these in order. For each one, quote the winner lines and loser lines
side by side, then write the difference (or "no difference"). Most real gaps sit in
lenses 1–4.

## 1. Hook wording (first sentence, first ~3 seconds)

- **The verb.** What action does the hook promise? ("turn X into", "use X to", "stop
  doing", "I tried"). Transformation verbs vs tool verbs vs effort verbs.
- **Subject.** "You" vs "I" vs "this" vs a third party. Who is the sentence about?
- **Specificity.** A number, a name, a dollar amount, a timeframe — present or not.
- **Outcome framing.** Gain vs loss avoidance; identity ("expert-level", "pro") vs
  task ("write a script").
- **Length.** Words before the first full stop.
- **Open loop.** Does the hook raise a question the video has to answer?

## 2. Hook-to-payoff distance

- At what second / sentence does the promised thing first appear?
- Is there a proof beat (result, screenshot, number) before the explanation?
- Do losers spend the opening on context ("so recently I've been…")?

## 3. Story structure

- Shape: problem → struggle → discovery → result, vs list, vs tutorial, vs opinion.
- Is there a concrete moment (a scene with a time, place or number) or only abstraction?
- Stakes: what does the viewer lose by not watching to the end?
- Contrast: before/after, best vs worst, expected vs actual.

## 4. Word-level patterns across the body

- Repeated phrases that only winners use (or only losers).
- Hedging ("kind of", "maybe", "I think") density.
- Jargon or insider terms the target viewer might not know.
- Sentence length and rhythm; run-on sentences in losers.
- Second-person address ("you") density.

## 5. Specificity and proof

- Real numbers (views, money, time saved) — count them per video.
- Named examples vs generic ones.
- Show vs tell: is the result demonstrated or just claimed?

## 6. Pacing and length

- Duration and words per second.
- Dead sections: places where nothing new is said for more than ~5 seconds.
- Where the transcript would likely lose people (long setup, repeated point).

## 7. Ending and CTA

- Does it end on the payoff or trail off?
- CTA type: comment keyword, follow, link — and whether it's earned by the content.
- A comment-keyword CTA ("comment AUDIT") can inflate comments and reach on its own —
  treat it as a possible confound.

## 8. Packaging (caption, on-screen text, topic)

- Caption first line vs the spoken hook — aligned or different?
- Topic/angle differences hiding inside a "same topic" set.

## Confounds to rule out before concluding

- Post date / account size at the time / posting time
- Trending sound or format
- External push (stitches, reposts, a big account sharing it)
- Duration differences big enough to change completion rate
- Platform differences if the set mixes TikTok, Reels and Shorts
